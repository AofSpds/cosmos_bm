## 1. Scoring method

Each concept is scored from 0 to 5 on seven axes.

| Axis | Weight | 0 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Existing BM problem relevance | 25% | no material BM problem | indirect or optional | directly closes a core BM gap |
| Technical transferability | 20% | domain-specific and non-transferable | transferable with substantial redesign | clear object/flow-level mapping |
| Data availability | 15% | required features/labels unavailable | partial or costly | observable, reproducible, freshness measurable |
| Safety and deterministic control | 15% | cannot be bounded | bounded with material unresolved risk | hard constraints, fallback and authorization are clear |
| Thin Core and neutrality fit | 10% | centralizes/competes with providers | mixed | preserves open provider competition and switching |
| Standards/research maturity | 10% | speculative | active research/PoC | in-force/published plus implementation evidence |
| Marketing defensibility | 5% | decorative/overclaim-prone | cautious claim possible | clear evidence-backed narrative |

Weighted score:

```text
0.25 × BM relevance
+ 0.20 × transferability
+ 0.15 × data
+ 0.15 × safety
+ 0.10 × neutrality
+ 0.10 × maturity
+ 0.05 × marketing defensibility
```

### 1.1 Decision rule

The numerical score is a screening aid, not an automatic verdict.

| Weighted score | Default screening |
|---:|---|
| 4.25–5.00 | SELECT candidate |
| 3.50–4.24 | SELECT-LIMITED or WATCH |
| 2.50–3.49 | WATCH or REJECT |
| below 2.50 | REJECT default |

### 1.2 Hard overrides

A concept can be rejected or held regardless of score when it causes any of the following:

- Hub centralization or direct competition with routers/wallets/providers;
- increased customer-principal or duplicate-execution risk;
- no deterministic baseline or reproducible measurement;
- no usable failure/recovery labels;
- AI control of keys, transaction authorization, hard policy or unilateral compensation;
- legal responsibility disproportionate to controllable events;
- cost exceeding measurable route/service benefit;
- a mainly decorative 6G claim with weak technical value.

## 3. Selection rationale

## 3.1 SELECT — mandatory roadmap foundation

### A. Normalized Observability / Evidence Plane

**Why selected**

- It is a prerequisite for BM1 attribution, BM2 completion/recovery/SLA, BM3 institutional reporting and BM4 provider history.
- Existing chain, packet, route and provider signals can be normalized without placing all analytics on Hub.
- It creates measurable evidence before AI or commercial assurance is introduced.

**Required scope**

```text
quote timestamp and expiry
source inclusion and finality
per-leg relay/swap/bridge state
destination inclusion and finality
realized amount, fee, gas and slippage
last-known asset location
recovery and compensation state
provider-controlled vs external event
source provenance and confidence
```

**Not included**

- raw KYC/PII;
- institution-sensitive transaction details on public chain;
- self-reported telemetry accepted without corroboration;
- a single indexer as the final commercial adjudicator.

### B. Interchain SLO/SLA Evidence Model

**Why selected**

- It directly operationalizes BM2’s Signed Quote, Provider Bond and Settlement/Recovery Receipt.
- It gives BM3 a usable report and BM4 a factual provider history.
- It transfers the assurance discipline rather than telecom’s radio-specific guarantee.

**Required distinction**

```text
Observed metric
→ service objective
→ signed commercial commitment
→ provider-controllable breach rule
→ exception rule
→ recovery state
→ bounded compensation rule
```

### C. Structured Service Intent / Capability Exposure

**Why selected**

- BM3’s value proposition is simpler access to multiple chains, routes and service providers.
- A machine-readable intent allows institutions and wallets to specify cost, completion, provider, asset, finality and recovery constraints without hard-coding each path.
- Capability records make provider competition auditable.

**Minimum objects**

```text
Service Intent
Service Class
Capability Record
Policy Profile
Route Constraint
SLA Template
Receipt Profile
Recovery Policy
```

**Initial service classes**

```text
STANDARD
LOW-COST
FAST
RISK-ADJUSTED
ASSURED
INSTITUTIONAL
```

## 3.2 SELECT-LIMITED — useful only inside hard boundaries

### A. Bounded AI-assisted Control Loop

AI is selected only for prediction, anomaly detection and candidate ranking. The optimizer and hard policy engine remain deterministic; wallet or institutional authorization remains explicit.

**Authorized research outputs**

- latency distribution estimate;
- realized-output/slippage estimate;
- failure and recovery probability;
- provider anomaly/health signal;
- risk-adjusted candidate score;
- human-readable explanation draft.

**Not authorized**

- key custody;
- transaction approval;
- bridge allowlist mutation;
- exposure-cap or minimum-receive override;
- finality weakening;
- compensation decision;
- live policy mutation.

### B. Predictive Assurance / Provider Health

Selected for M2 shadow evaluation only until data-gate evidence exists. Model output must carry version, feature time, confidence and abstention. Provider rank cannot equal eligibility; eligibility is deterministic and separately governed.

### C. Sequential Failover

Selected in this order:

```text
candidate route diversity
→ pre-execution provider/client/channel health check
→ deterministic route replacement before user authorization
→ bounded post-authorization retry where idempotency is provable
→ only later consider any mid-flight mechanism
```

### D. Provider Conflict Detection / Deterministic Arbitration

Selected for:

- conflicting route/provider claims;
- overlapping asset representation;
- incompatible intent constraints;
- stale or inconsistent provider capability;
- model disagreement exceeding confidence threshold;
- policy priority and concentration rules.

Final arbitration uses a published deterministic rule and audit trail, not a hidden model decision.

### E. Natural-language Intent Assistant

Selected only as an optional drafting layer. It may help a user produce a structured request, but the structured fields, detected conflicts, assumptions, fee/risk/finality/recovery disclosure and explicit confirmation are authoritative.

## 3.3 Existing / assess — do not relabel as new

### Candidate Route Diversity

Current routing already evaluates asset origin, IBC denom/path, channel/client state and liquidity. This becomes the deterministic comparison baseline and the source of candidate routes. The roadmap adds assurance and evidence around it; it does not claim to invent it.

### Smart-Swap Split Routing

Current split routing is relevant evidence that multi-route allocation can improve output in some swaps. The roadmap must separately measure:

```text
incremental output
- extra fee
- extra gas
- liquidity impact
- recovery cost
- capital cost
= net route value
```

A split swap is not evidence that redundant cross-chain execution is safe.

---

## 3.4 WATCH — preserve research optionality without programme dependency

### Historical Replay / Simulation

Use historical replay, synthetic route failures, provider outages and exposure simulations before using the term “digital twin.” Promotion requires measured predictive validity and explicit model limits.

### Agentic / Multi-agent Orchestration

Maintain as an off-chain research track. Promotion requires:

- explicit agent roles and least privilege;
- conflict and deadlock handling;
- deterministic authorization boundary;
- full tool-call and evidence trace;
- predictable latency/cost;
- no critical-path dependence.

### Redundant/Hedged Execution

Default off. It is promoted only if all of the following are proven:

1. duplicate settlement is prevented;
2. per-leg state and asset location are recoverable;
3. aggregate completion is unambiguous;
4. added cost is smaller than measured tail-risk reduction;
5. regulatory/accounting treatment is acceptable;
6. provider and route exposure remain within limits.

Otherwise it is killed without affecting the rest of the roadmap.

## 7. Dependency and promotion rules

```text
Observability/Evidence
        ↓
Metric and SLO taxonomy
        ↓
Deterministic baseline
        ↓
Shadow prediction and calibration
        ↓
Bounded deterministic SLA pilot
        ↓
Multi-provider and limited failover evaluation
        ↓
Structured enterprise abstraction
        ↓
Adaptive scale only after safety, neutrality and economics pass
```

### 7.1 Non-dependencies

- BM1 Distribution MVP **does not depend on** AI route prediction.
- Structured-intent UX may be prototyped before BM3, but **does not authorize execution**.
- Route diversity exists before the proposed SLA market; it is **not proof of commercial assurance**.
- Multi-path and agentic research can be killed independently without blocking observability, SLO/SLA or BM3 capability work.
- No selected concept requires a fifth BM or immediate native SDK module.

## 8. Go / Hold / Kill summary by selected concept

| Concept | Start condition | PASS | HOLD | KILL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Observability / Evidence | source/event inventory complete | timestamp/provenance/reconciliation coverage meets threshold | material missing leg or source disagreement | evidence cannot be reproduced or privacy boundary cannot be met |
| SLO/SLA model | evidence schema and controllability taxonomy | metrics are measurable; exception and breach rules reproducible | legal/economic terms unresolved | commitment cannot be objectively adjudicated or exposure is unbounded |
| Structured intent | capability fields and policy constraints defined | invalid/conflicting intent fails closed; user sees risk and confirms | excessive ambiguity or unsupported capability | abstraction hides material route/finality/recovery risk |
| AI prediction | deterministic baselines frozen | out-of-sample calibrated improvement and safe abstention | drift, label scarcity or unstable gain | no material benefit over baseline or unsafe behavior |
| Predictive provider health | independent data and concentration metrics | calibrated risk signal improves outcomes without concentration harm | confidence too wide or self-report dominates | discrimination/monopoly feedback cannot be controlled |
| Sequential failover | unique ID and stage machine | no duplicate execution; output and recovery remain within policy | uncertain asset location or expired commitment | duplicate/stranded-asset risk exceeds benefit |
| Conflict arbitration | signed inputs and public priorities | reproducible resolution and audit trail | unresolved multi-policy collision | centralized opaque arbiter becomes de facto monopoly |
| Natural-language assistant | fixed schema and safe parser boundary | structured output passes validation and explicit confirmation | unsupported/ambiguous request | direct or misleading execution path emerges |
